No two people respond to exercise the same way.

Exercise Mechanics™ is the study of how resistance acts on the body during exercise, and how that shapes the stimulus, what the body can tolerate, and the outcome.

A trainer who understands how a muscle produces force at each joint position, and how the resistance is acting on the body, can work out what each client needs from an exercise.

There are no methods, just means of understanding things and testing whether interventions have had a positive, intentional effect.

Where RTS comes from

1989
In 1989 the founder of NASM brought in Tom Purvis, who had coined Exercise Mechanics, to write the biomechanics lectures and the hands-on part of the first personal training certification in the US.
1997
RTS became a course of its own in 1997, and our Chief Education Officer was teaching it around the world that same year.

The RTS levels

Each level builds on the one before, and asks you to solve harder problems with your clients.

  1. 1

    RTS Level 1

    Level 1 teaches how force and resistance act on the body, and how bodies differ from one person to the next, so you can analyze any exercise for whoever is doing it.

    You study online and in a live practical with an RTS instructor, then take an exam.
